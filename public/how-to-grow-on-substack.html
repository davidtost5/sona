How to grow on Substack, step by step.

Seven things writers who grow on Substack tend to do, in the order that matters most when you’re starting out, plus a weekly routine you can keep.

By David Tost, maker of Sona. Published 15 November 2026.

Growing on Substack is slower than people expect for the first few months, then faster than they expect once the pieces connect. The writers who grow tend to do the same handful of things: publish on a schedule, use Notes, get recommended by other writers, and give every post a reason to be shared.

Here is how to do each of those, in the order that matters most when you are starting out.

1. Pick a promise a reader can repeat

People subscribe to a clear promise, not to a person they haven’t heard of yet. “A weekly breakdown of one pricing page that works” is easier to recommend than “thoughts on business”. Write your promise in one sentence and put it in your publication’s description, your about page and your welcome email.

If you are not sure what to write about, our list of Substack newsletter ideas has formats and topics to start from. Brand new to Substack? Start with getting started on Substack.

2. Publish on a schedule you can keep

Readers trust a newsletter that shows up. One good post a week, on the same day, beats three posts one week and none the next. Choose a rhythm you could keep during your busiest month, and treat it as fixed. Writing ahead and scheduling your posts makes this much easier.

3. Use Notes every day

Notes is Substack’s short-form feed, and for most new writers it is the biggest source of new subscribers inside Substack. Share one idea per note, reply thoughtfully to writers in your niche, and restack posts you genuinely liked with a line on why. Our guide to Substack Notes covers how they differ from posts and what to share.

4. Get recommended by other writers

When a publication recommends yours, new subscribers to it are invited to subscribe to you too. That makes recommendations one of the most reliable ways to grow.

  • Recommend newsletters you actually read. Many writers return the favour once they notice.
  • Comment and restack before you ask for anything. Writers recommend people they recognise.
  • Suggest a collaboration: a guest post, a joint interview or a swap of reading lists.

5. Write titles and openings that earn the click

Your title does the work in the inbox, in Notes and in search. Make it specific: who it is for and what they get. Then make the first paragraph keep the promise straight away, without a warm-up.

  • The 4 emails I send every new client in their first weekSpecific, useful, and easy to forward.
  • I tracked every hour for 30 days. Here’s where the week actually went.A personal experiment with a result.
  • Why most pricing pages bury the one number buyers wantA clear opinion about a common mistake.

These titles were written for this guide to show the pattern.

6. Study the posts that beat their author’s average

Every writer in your niche has a few posts that did far better than usual. Those are the most useful posts to study, because the author, audience and timing are the same as always: the difference is the idea or how it was written. Note the title, the opening and the format of each, and look for patterns.

You can do this by hand by checking the likes, comments and restacks on a writer’s recent posts. Sona does it for you across writers in your niche.

7. Bring readers in from outside Substack

  • Turn each post into one short post for LinkedIn or X, with a link at the end. Our list of LinkedIn hook examples helps with the opening.
  • Add your newsletter link to your email signature and social profiles.
  • Make your best posts easy to find: pin them and link them from your about page.

A weekly routine for growing on Substack

  • Every day, 15 minutes: one note, and thoughtful replies to two or three writers in your niche.
  • Once a week: publish your post on the same day, then share it in a note and on one other platform.
  • Once a month: look at your three best posts, work out why they did better, and reach out to one writer about a recommendation or collaboration.

Questions people ask

How long does it take to grow on Substack?
Most newsletters grow slowly for the first few months. Consistent publishing, daily Notes and recommendations from other writers are what usually change the pace. Judge progress over quarters, not weeks.
How do I get readers on Substack with no audience?
Start inside Substack: post Notes, reply to writers in your niche and recommend publications you read. Then bring people in from outside with short posts on LinkedIn or X that link back to your newsletter.
How do I get discovered on Substack?
Notes, recommendations from other publications and restacks are the main ways readers find new writers inside Substack. Clear titles also help your posts get found in search.
How often should I post on Substack?
Pick a schedule you can keep, usually once a week. Consistency matters more than frequency, and Notes can fill the days in between.
