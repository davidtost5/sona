23 Substack ideas for your newsletter.

Formats you can repeat, topics only you can write, posts for slow weeks, and a few ways to name the whole thing.

By David Tost, maker of Sona. Published 15 November 2026.

The hardest part of a newsletter isn’t the first post. It’s the twentieth. The writers who keep going usually have a repeatable format and a list of ideas to pull from on slow weeks.

Here are 23 Substack ideas: formats you can repeat, topics drawn from your own experience, single posts for when you are stuck, and a few ways to name the whole thing.

23 Substack newsletter ideas

Formats you can repeat every week

  • A weekly breakdown of one thing that works in your field: a page, an email, a deal or a post.
  • Three links worth reading, with a paragraph each on why.
  • An interview with one person in your niche, five questions long.
  • A teardown of a mistake: what happened, why, and what to do instead.
  • A monthly report on your own numbers, wins and misses included.
  • Reader questions, answered in full.
  • A glossary entry: one term from your field, explained properly.
  • A case study of one person, company or project.

Topics drawn from your own experience

  • Lessons from your job that nobody taught you in training.
  • A skill you are learning, documented week by week.
  • A project from start to finish, with the parts that went wrong.
  • The tools and templates you actually use.
  • Advice you disagree with, and what you do instead.
  • Stories from clients or colleagues, shared with permission.
  • A field you know well, explained for people outside it.

Single post ideas when you are stuck

  • The best thing you read this month, and what you took from it.
  • A post answering the question you get asked most.
  • The five mistakes you see most often in your niche.
  • Something you changed your mind about this year.
  • A list of resources for someone just starting out.
  • A behind-the-scenes look at how you make the newsletter.
  • A prediction for your field, with your reasoning.
  • A collaboration: swap a post with another writer.

Ideas for your Substack name

  • Your promise in a few words, such as the result readers get.
  • Your name plus the topic, which is clear and easy to search.
  • A phrase from your field that insiders will recognise.
  • A number or rhythm, such as how often it arrives or how long it takes to read.

How to choose your Substack topic

  1. Pick something you could write about for a year. If you can list 20 posts today, the topic has enough room.
  2. Pick a reader, not just a subject. “Pricing for first-time founders” is easier to grow than “pricing”.
  3. Check what already works. Find writers in that space and look at which of their posts did far better than usual. That shows you what readers want more of.

What to post on Substack between newsletters

Use Notes. Share one idea from your latest post, ask your readers a question, or restack another writer with a line on why it’s worth reading. Our guide to Substack Notes covers what works, and how to grow on Substack covers the rest.

Questions people ask

What should I write about on Substack?
Something you know well and could write about for at least a year, aimed at a specific reader. Your own work, experiments and opinions are the easiest material to keep going with.
What kind of Substack newsletters do well?
Ones with a clear promise and a repeatable format, such as a weekly breakdown, a curated list with commentary or a series of case studies. Look at which posts outperform in your niche to see what readers want.
How do I come up with a Substack name?
Say what readers get in a few words, or pair your name with your topic. Keep it easy to spell and to search.
What do I post on Substack besides newsletters?
Notes: short posts that share one idea, a quote from your latest post, a question or a restack with your comment.
