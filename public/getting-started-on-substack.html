Getting started on Substack, from zero to first post.

What to decide before you start, how to set up your publication, how to publish your first post, and where your first readers come from.

By David Tost, maker of Sona. Published 28 November 2026.

Starting on Substack takes an afternoon. The setup is simple; the decisions that matter are what you promise readers and how often you’ll show up. This guide walks through both, from an empty account to your first published post.

Before you set anything up

  • Write your promise in one sentence: who it’s for and what they get. “A weekly breakdown of one pricing page that works, for first-time founders.”
  • Pick a rhythm you can keep in your busiest month. Weekly is a good default.
  • List ten post ideas. If you can’t, narrow the topic. Our Substack newsletter ideas help.

Set up your publication

  1. Create your account on Substack with the email you want readers to reply to.
  2. Start a publication. Give it a name readers can repeat (see Substack name ideas) and a web address you won’t want to change later.
  3. Write a one-line description using your promise. It shows when people find you and when they’re asked to subscribe.
  4. Fill in your about page: what you write, how often, and why you’re the one writing it. See about page examples.
  5. Write a welcome email for new subscribers, with your best post or a short “start here”. See welcome email examples.
  6. Add a photo and a short bio to your profile. Your profile is you; your publication is the newsletter. People see both.

How to make your first post on Substack

  1. Open your dashboard and start a new post.
  2. Write a specific title: who it’s for and what they get. Add a subtitle that finishes the thought.
  3. Keep the first paragraph to the point. Readers decide in a few lines whether to keep going.
  4. Send yourself a test email and read it on your phone.
  5. Choose who it goes to (everyone, or the web only) and publish now or schedule it.

Find your first readers

  • Tell people you already know, one by one, with a link to a specific post rather than your homepage.
  • Start using Substack Notes: one note a day and thoughtful replies to writers in your niche.
  • Recommend newsletters you read. It’s how many writers first notice you.
  • Share each post once outside Substack, for example on LinkedIn or X, with a short summary and the link.

Once you’re publishing regularly, how to grow on Substack covers what to do next.

Substack profile vs publication

Your profile is you: your name, photo, bio and notes. A publication is a newsletter, with its own name, address and subscribers. One profile can write for more than one publication, and readers can follow your profile without subscribing to a publication.

Common first-month mistakes

  • Spending a week on the design and no time on the first five posts.
  • Publishing three times in week one, then going quiet.
  • Writing for everyone. A clear reader makes every decision easier.
  • Judging the newsletter by the first month’s numbers. Growth is slow at first for almost everyone.

Questions people ask

How do I get started on Substack?
Write a one-sentence promise for your readers, create an account, start a publication with a clear name and description, fill in your about page and welcome email, then publish your first post and share it.
How do I make a post on Substack?
Start a new post from your dashboard, write a specific title and subtitle, send yourself a test email, then publish to everyone or to the web only, now or at a scheduled time.
Is Substack free to start?
Yes, a free newsletter on Substack costs nothing. If you turn on paid subscriptions, Substack and the payment processor take a share of what you earn.
What is the difference between a Substack profile and a publication?
Your profile is you: name, photo, bio and notes. A publication is a newsletter with its own name, address and subscribers. One profile can write for more than one publication.
