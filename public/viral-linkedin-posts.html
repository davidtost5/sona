Viral LinkedIn posts, and why they work.

The posts worth studying aren’t the ones with the biggest numbers. They’re the ones that beat their author’s usual result by the widest margin. Here’s how to find them and what to learn from them.

By David Tost, maker of Sona. Published 15 November 2026.

Most lists of “viral LinkedIn posts” show you the same handful of screenshots from accounts with a million followers. That is the least useful place to learn from. A post with 20,000 likes from someone that size might be a normal Tuesday for them. A post with 900 likes from someone who usually gets 60 is the one worth studying.

This guide covers what “viral” should mean, how to find those posts in your own niche, the patterns they tend to share, and how to write your own version without copying anyone.

What counts as a viral LinkedIn post

Judge a post against its author, not against the whole platform. The useful question is: how far did this post beat what this person usually gets?

  • Raw numbers mislead. Big accounts get big numbers on average posts. Their best ideas and their filler look the same from the outside.
  • Outliers teach. When a post does three, five or ten times its author’s normal engagement, something about the idea or the way it was written made the difference. That something is what you want to learn.
  • Comments matter more than likes. A post that starts a conversation keeps getting shown. Likes are cheap; a thread of real replies is not.

How to find viral LinkedIn posts in your niche

  1. Pick ten to twenty creators in your niche, at roughly your size or a little bigger. Skip the celebrities: their audience reacts to who they are, not what they wrote.
  2. Learn each one’s normal. Scroll their last 20 posts and note a typical number of reactions and comments. You only need a rough figure.
  3. Flag the outliers. Any post at three times their normal or more goes in your list. Most creators have one or two a month.
  4. Use LinkedIn search for topics. Search a topic, switch to Posts, and sort by Latest to see what is landing this week rather than what was popular last year.
  5. Save them somewhere you will reopen. A swipe file you never read is just a graveyard. Keep the post, the creator’s normal, and one line on why you think it worked.

Five patterns behind most viral LinkedIn posts

Topics change from niche to niche, but the shape of a post that travels is surprisingly consistent. These five come up again and again. The example hooks below are ones we wrote to show the pattern, not quotes from real posts.

1. The reversal

Start from a belief your reader holds, then turn it around. The tension makes people read the second line.

  • I stopped posting every day. My reach went up.Common belief: more posts, more reach.

2. The specific story

One moment, told with real detail: a date, a number, a sentence someone said. Specific beats general every time.

  • At 9:40 on a Monday my biggest client emailed one line: “We’re pausing.”Time, stakes and a quote in eleven words.

3. The list with a promise

A numbered list works when the first line says exactly what the reader gets and why it is worth their minute.

  • 7 questions I ask before every hire. Number 4 has saved me twice.A count, a use and a reason to reach the middle.

4. The confession

Admitting a mistake or a bad habit earns trust quickly, as long as the post ends with something the reader can use.

  • I sent 400 cold emails last quarter. Here’s the embarrassing reason only 3 got replies.

5. The sharp opinion

A clear, slightly uncomfortable position invites people to agree or argue in the comments, and both keep the post alive.

  • Most “personal brand” advice is just posting more. That’s why it doesn’t work.

How to write your own version without copying

The goal is to borrow the shape, never the words or the story. A simple way to do that:

  1. Name the pattern. Is it a reversal, a story, a list, a confession or an opinion?
  2. Strip it to a skeleton. “Common belief, then the opposite result, then what I learned.”
  3. Fill it with your own material. Your numbers, your client, your mistake. If you have no real story that fits, pick a different post to learn from.
  4. Write the first two lines last. Draft the body, then write three openings and keep the one you would stop scrolling for.

A 20-minute weekly routine

  • 10 minutes: find three outliers in your niche and save them.
  • 5 minutes: name the pattern behind each.
  • 5 minutes: write one opening of your own for each pattern. One of them becomes this week’s post.

Questions people ask

What counts as a viral post on LinkedIn?
There is no official number. A more useful definition is a post that did several times better than its author normally does. Three times the usual reactions and comments is a good line to start with.
How do I find the most viral LinkedIn posts in my niche?
Follow 10 to 20 creators of a similar size, learn what a normal post gets for each, and save the posts that do three times that or more. LinkedIn search sorted by Latest helps you find what is working this week. Sona does this tracking for you.
Is it okay to copy a viral LinkedIn post?
Copying the words or the story is not. Borrowing the structure is how most good writers learn: keep the shape, and fill it with your own experience and numbers.
Do hashtags make LinkedIn posts go viral?
Rarely on their own. The first two lines decide whether people expand the post, and comments decide how far it travels. Treat hashtags as a small extra, not the strategy.
