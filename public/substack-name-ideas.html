24 Substack name ideas, and how to choose.

Clear names get recommended. Here are examples in four styles, a quick way to pick yours, and how to choose a username to match.

By David Tost, maker of Sona. Published 18 November 2026.

Your Substack name does two jobs: it tells a stranger what they’ll get, and it’s the thing a happy reader has to remember when they recommend you. Clever names often fail the first job. Clear names rarely fail either.

Below are 24 Substack name ideas in four styles, then a quick way to pick yours and choose a username to go with it. The names are examples written for this guide; check that one is free before you use it.

24 Substack name ideas

  • Ten-Minute Marketing

How to choose a Substack name

  1. Start from your promise. Write what readers get in one sentence, then shorten it. “A weekly breakdown of one pricing page” becomes “The Weekly Teardown”.
  2. Say it out loud. If you have to spell it for someone, it will be hard to recommend.
  3. Search it. Check Substack, Google and the social networks you use, so you aren’t sharing a name with someone bigger.
  4. Leave room to grow. A name tied to one tool or one year can box you in later.

Substack username and handle ideas

Your handle is how people find and mention you, so keep it close to your real name or your publication’s name. Short, lowercase and without numbers is easiest. If your name is taken, add your topic (mayaonproduct) rather than random digits.

Questions people ask

How do I name my Substack?
Write what readers get in one sentence, then shorten it into a few words that are easy to say and spell. Pairing your own name with your topic is a safe choice.
Can I change my Substack name later?
Yes, you can rename your publication in its settings. Changing your web address is a bigger step, because old links may stop working, so choose that carefully.
Should my Substack be my own name?
Your own name works well if you are the reason people read. A topic name works better if you might add writers later or want strangers to understand it at a glance.
What makes a good Substack username?
Short, lowercase, close to your real or publication name, and without random numbers, so people can find and mention you easily.
