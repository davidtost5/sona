Substack Notes, and how to use them.

What notes are, how they differ from posts, and how to use them to bring new readers to your newsletter.

By David Tost, maker of Sona. Published 15 November 2026.

Substack Notes is the short-form side of Substack: a feed of quick posts, links, quotes and images from the writers and readers you follow. It looks a lot like a social network, and for many newsletter writers it has become the main way new readers find them.

What are notes on Substack?

A note is a short post that appears in the Notes feed, in the Substack app and on your profile. You can write a few lines, share an image or a link, quote part of a post, or restack someone else’s note or post with your own comment. Readers can like, reply to and restack notes, which is how they spread to people who don’t follow you yet.

Substack Notes vs posts

The short version: posts are your newsletter; notes are the conversation around it.

  • Posts are full articles. They are emailed to your subscribers and live on your publication.
  • Notes are short. They are not emailed to subscribers; they show up in the Notes feed and app, where people who don’t subscribe to you can see them too.
  • Posts build depth with people who already chose you. Notes help new people discover you in the first place.

The two work best together: a note can share the best idea from a post, and a post can grow out of a note that started a good conversation.

How to use Substack Notes to grow

  1. One idea per note. A single clear thought travels further than a summary of everything you know.
  2. Lead with the point. The first line decides whether people stop, just like a post title. Our list of hook examples works on Notes too.
  3. Reply more than you post. Thoughtful replies on other writers’ notes put your name in front of their readers.
  4. Restack with a reason. Add a line on why something is worth reading. A bare restack says nothing about you.
  5. Share from your posts. A strong quote, chart or idea from your latest post makes a good note, with a link for people who want the rest.

What to post on Substack Notes

  • One lesson from this week, in three lines.
  • A quote from your latest post, with the context in one sentence.
  • A question your readers can answer from experience.
  • A short opinion about something happening in your niche.
  • A restack of another writer’s post, with what you took from it.
  • A behind-the-scenes look at what you’re writing next.
  • A small chart, screenshot or list that stands on its own.

How often to post notes

There is no official number. Many writers post one to three notes a day and spend as much time replying as posting. If that is too much, one good note a day and a few thoughtful replies is a strong habit to start with.

Learn from notes and posts that outperform

The fastest way to get better at Notes is to look at what already works in your niche. When a writer’s note or post gets far more likes, replies and restacks than they usually do, look at the first line, the format and the topic. For the bigger picture, see how to grow on Substack.

Questions people ask

What is Substack Notes?
Substack Notes is a feed of short posts inside Substack. Writers and readers share quick thoughts, links, quotes and images, and can like, reply to and restack each other’s notes.
What is the difference between notes and posts on Substack?
Posts are full articles emailed to your subscribers. Notes are short, are not emailed, and appear in the Notes feed and app, where people who don’t subscribe to you can find them.
Do Substack Notes help you get subscribers?
Yes, for many writers they are a main source of new subscribers inside Substack, because notes and restacks reach people who don’t follow you yet.
Should I use hashtags on Substack Notes?
They are not how Notes is usually discovered. A clear first line, replies and restacks do far more to get a note seen.
