Substack about page examples.

Where a curious stranger decides whether to subscribe. A simple structure, three examples to adapt, and the mistakes that cost you readers.

By David Tost, maker of Sona. Published 28 November 2026.

Your about page is where a curious stranger decides whether to subscribe. They want three answers fast: what is this, is it for me, and why should I trust this person. A good about page answers all three before the first scroll.

A simple structure for your about page

  1. The promise, in one line. Who it’s for and what they get.
  2. How often it arrives, and whether any of it is paid.
  3. Why you: two or three sentences of real experience, not a full biography.
  4. Your best posts: three links, so people can judge the writing for themselves.
  5. A clear invitation to subscribe.

Substack about page examples

These examples were written for this guide to show the structure.

For a niche, practical newsletter

  • Every Thursday, one pricing page from a real company, taken apart: what it gets right, what it gets wrong, and what you can copy. For founders and marketers who set prices. I’ve run pricing for three software companies and still get it wrong sometimes, which is why I study it. Start with: [post], [post], [post].

For a personal essay newsletter

  • Essays about changing careers at forty, written while I do it. Every other Sunday, free. I left accounting last spring to become a carpenter; this is the honest version of how that’s going. Most-read so far: [post], [post], [post].

For a curated newsletter

  • Five links about design worth your time, every Friday, each with a paragraph on why. I read about 200 articles a week as a design lead so you don’t have to. No filler, no sponsored picks without a label.

About page mistakes to avoid

  • Starting with your life story instead of what readers get.
  • Being vague about how often you publish.
  • No links to posts, so readers can’t judge the writing.
  • Leaving it unchanged for a year while the newsletter changes.

Your welcome email picks up where the about page leaves off, and a clear publication name makes both easier to write.

Questions people ask

What should I put on my Substack about page?
Your promise in one line, how often you publish, a few sentences on why you are the person writing it, links to three of your best posts, and a clear invitation to subscribe.
How long should a Substack about page be?
Short enough to read in under a minute. Most good about pages are a few short paragraphs plus links to posts.
Should my about page be about me or the newsletter?
Lead with the newsletter and what readers get, then add a little about you to show why you are worth reading.
How often should I update my about page?
Whenever your best posts or your schedule change, and at least every few months.
