Sona is made by one person.

Sona is an independent tool built by David Tost. There is no team, office or investor behind it: just the product, and the person who answers your email.

Why Sona exists

Most advice about what to post is guesswork. Sona starts from evidence instead: public posts on X and LinkedIn that did far better than their creator usually does, and how each one is built. You learn the pattern, then write your own version in your own words. Sona never writes your posts for you.

What Sona will and won't do

Every post in Sona is a real, public post, linked to the original. Each one is measured against its own creator's usual numbers, not against the biggest accounts, so a writer with two thousand followers can teach you as much as one with two million. When Sona decodes a post, it names the hook, the turn, where the post lands and the line you can reuse.

What it won't do is write the post for you. Zeno, the research partner inside the app, will find posts, compare them and critique your draft, but the words you publish stay yours. Your playbook, the record of how you sound and what you'd never say, sits beside every draft to keep it that way.

How it gets better

Sona ships small changes often, and every one is listed in the changelog, newest first. Most of them start with an email: something that was confusing, a post someone wanted to study and couldn't, a step that took too long. If you use Sona and something is in your way, that's the most useful thing you can send.

How it is run

Sona is run by David Tost as an individual; it is not a registered company yet. Payments go through Polar as merchant of record, sign-in through Clerk, and your work is stored in Supabase. The privacy policy and terms have the details.
